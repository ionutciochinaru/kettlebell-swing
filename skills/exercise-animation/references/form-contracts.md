# Source-backed motion contracts

Use the rows relevant to the assigned exercise. These are starting points, not a complete exercise library or medical prescription. Exact primary pages were opened on 2026-09-24. Reopen the relevant pages for new implementation or disputed details. Study their instruction and demonstrations; public availability does not grant rights to redistribute photographs or video.

## Contract record

For each motion, record:

- Exercise name and represented variant; what one rep or one hold means.
- Source URL, section, checked date, facts supported, and unresolved differences.
- Start → load/prepare → action → reversal/hold → return; both sides when required.
- Ground/support contacts per phase and intentional releases; equipment attachment.
- Which joints move, which segments stabilize, and what the chosen camera must reveal.
- Intended loop duration and frame count; phases needing longer presentation.
- Concrete misleading errors to reject, without inventing universal angles.

Label camera, frame count, exact pose dimensions and easing as animation choices. A source's example depth or exercise dose does not become a universal requirement. If two reputable sources describe different variants, select and label the intended one instead of blending incompatible poses.

## Known source caveats

The NASM push-up and plank pages contained an unrelated repeated barbell-deadlift paragraph when checked. Use their named exercise sections and corroborate disputed instruction with another primary source. The Illinois page uses maximal-pull language; this is not a reason to replace a product's gentle light-band default. The Oxford lunge is explicitly chair-supported. Different hand spacing, gaze cues, depths and per-side repetition conventions may describe legitimate variants.

## Frame defects that identify a bad motion model

These are engineering deductions to inspect in the rendered result:

- If a curl's wrist follows a straight line through its elbow, the forearm may collapse mid-cycle even when both endpoints look plausible.
- A fixed camera can change projected segment length through foreshortening; world-space bone length must still remain constant. Do not “fix” projection by stretching anatomy.
- If both feet translate on the floor at constant spacing, the motion is skating, not a side step.
- If a reverse lunge moves the front support foot, the body may be morphing between silhouettes instead of taking a step.
- Forefoot contact distinguishes a calf raise from whole-body levitation.
- A held object can look attached in one frame yet separate during another. Check both hands throughout the whole grip phase.
- A mirror-image replay is not automatically an opposite-side repetition: verify side labels, support contacts and camera orientation remain consistent.
